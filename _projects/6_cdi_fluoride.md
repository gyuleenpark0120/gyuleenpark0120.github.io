## Overview

Excess fluoride in drinking water is a global environmental health problem, particularly in regions where groundwater fluoride concentrations exceed WHO guidelines. Capacitive deionization (CDI) offers an energy-efficient electrochemical route to water treatment, but achieving ionic selectivity – removing fluoride preferentially over competing anions – requires deliberate materials design.

This research developed a reduced graphene oxide / hydroxyapatite (rGO/HA) composite electrode that exploits the surface chemistry of hydroxyapatite to achieve selective fluoride capture, while the rGO scaffold provides the electrical conductivity and surface area needed for practical CDI operation.

---

## Contributions

**Composite Electrode Synthesis**
Synthesized rGO/HA composites via hydrothermal routes, controlling rGO-to-HA ratios and surface functionalization to tune both conductivity and fluoride-binding capacity.

**Selectivity Mechanism Analysis**
Characterized the fluoride selectivity mechanism: hydroxyapatite undergoes a fluorapatite substitution reaction (OH⁻ → F⁻ exchange at surface sites), providing a chemical affinity for fluoride beyond simple electrostatic accumulation. Confirmed the composite structure through FE-TEM and XRD analysis, and evaluated electrochemical selectivity via cyclic voltammetry.

**Electrochemical Performance Evaluation**
Evaluated CDI performance (adsorption capacity, regenerability, selectivity over Cl⁻ and NO₃⁻) under relevant water chemistry conditions, connecting materials properties to practical removal efficiency metrics.
