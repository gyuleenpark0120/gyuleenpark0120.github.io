Nature 2023 paper ↗

Scaling deep learning for materials discovery

DeepMind's GNoME used GNNs trained on DFT stability data to predict stable inorganic crystal structures at scale, expanding known stable candidates from ~48,000 to ~380,000 and enabling high-throughput pre-screening before any experiment.

What this paper does
The traditional way to discover new materials is to make them in the lab. Time-consuming and expensive. GNoME trains a GNN on DFT stability data to predict whether a given crystal structure will be stable, screening out poor candidates before any experiment is run. DeepMind expanded known stable candidates from ~48,000 to ~380,000 (roughly 8x). Some have since been synthesized and experimentally confirmed. The scale of the search space that can be explored changed meaningfully.
Takeaway
◆ Scale is the contribution. 380,000 candidates is more than any lab could verify in a lifetime, but generating a large pool of high-probability candidates raises the hit rate of experiments dramatically. When the size of the search space changes, what's discoverable changes with it.
◇ GNoME's definition of "stable" is DFT-computed stability, which is not the same as experimentally synthesizable. The gap between computational prediction and actual synthesis must be one of the most important open problems in the field right now.
