Selective Fluoride Removal via Capacitive Deionization

Synthesized reduced graphene oxide/hydroxyapatite composite electrodes for capacitive deionization, achieving selective fluoride removal from water. Connected materials synthesis and surface chemistry design with environmental application constraints.

Overview

Excess fluoride in drinking water is a global environmental health problem, particularly in regions where groundwater fluoride concentrations exceed WHO guidelines. Capacitive deionization (CDI) offers an energy-efficient electrochemical route to water treatment, but achieving ionic selectivity – removing fluoride preferentially over competing anions – requires deliberate materials design.

This project developed a reduced graphene oxide / hydroxyapatite (rGO/HAP) composite electrode that exploits the surface chemistry of hydroxyapatite to achieve selective fluoride capture, while the rGO scaffold provides the electrical conductivity and surface area needed for practical CDI operation.

What I Did

Composite Electrode Synthesis Synthesized rGO/HAP composites via hydrothermal routes, controlling rGO-to-HAP ratios and surface functionalization to tune both conductivity and fluoride-binding capacity.

Selectivity Mechanism Analysis Characterized the fluoride selectivity mechanism: hydroxyapatite undergoes a fluorapatite substitution reaction (OH⁻ → F⁻ exchange at surface sites), providing a chemical affinity for fluoride beyond simple electrostatic accumulation. Confirmed the mechanism through XRD, FT-IR, and XPS analysis of pre- and post-adsorption electrode surfaces.

Electrochemical Performance Evaluation Evaluated CDI performance (adsorption capacity, regenerability, selectivity over Cl⁻ and NO₃⁻) under relevant water chemistry conditions, connecting materials properties to practical removal efficiency metrics.
